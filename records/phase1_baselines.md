# Phase 1: baselines and diagnostics (before any new allocator)

Hardware: 4 CPU cores, no GPU (`records/environment.md`). All numbers below are from this sandbox.
Raw results: `results/phase1/`. Tables are generated by `python tools/summarize_phase1.py`.
Unless stated, "+/-" is the standard error over 5 seeds; paired differences use the same seed
(same weight initialisation and minibatch order).

## Summary in plain language

**D3 (spiking, SHD, 128 recurrent LIF units)**
1. Our tuned homogeneous network reaches 74.33 +/- 0.63 % test accuracy. Perez-Nieves et al. report
   71.7 +/- 1.0 %. That part reproduces (we are 2.6 points higher).
2. Learning all time constants reaches 75.68 +/- 0.55 %, a gain of only +1.35 +/- 0.89 points
   (4 of 5 seeds). Perez-Nieves report 82.7 +/- 0.8 %, a gain of +11 points. **We do not reproduce
   the size of the learned-time-constant gain; we are 7.0 points below their number.** Likely reasons
   (not tested): our time step is 10 ms against their 0.5 ms, and we have no separate synaptic time
   constant, so our neurons have less temporal resolution to exploit. A second tuning pass (channel-shift
   augmentation, 6x larger time-constant learning rate; 2 extra seeds, exploratory) raised test accuracy
   to 77-79 % but the gain from learning stayed at about 0-3 points.
3. Random heterogeneity gives no gain: +0.11 +/- 0.81 points (published: +2.0).
4. A mild spike-rate penalty on the homogeneous network cuts spikes 2.5x (4258 -> 1713 per sample) with
   no accuracy loss (75.59 +/- 0.76 %). The unpenalised networks are therefore wasteful, and any energy
   claim has to be judged against the penalised accuracy-vs-SynOps curve, not against the unpenalised baseline.
5. Diagnostic: per-neuron sensitivity to the time constant (Fisher-style) is **stable** (rank correlation
   0.94 across data halves, 0.91 train vs validation, 0.74 across two training runs from the same
   initialisation) but only **moderately concentrated**: Gini 0.51, top 10 % of neurons hold 39 %
   (a flat vector gives 10 %; a squared Gaussian gives about 44 %). It agrees only moderately with the
   measured loss change when one time constant is doubled or halved (rank correlation 0.41), and for
   22 % of neurons that perturbation lowers the loss. So the premise "a few neurons carry most of the
   sensitivity" is weakly supported here: the score is real and reproducible, but not heavy-tailed.
   It is not flat or unstable, so the allocator test goes ahead, with a low ceiling: the most any
   allocation of learnable time constants can gain in this harness is about the +1.35 points of `learned_tau`.

**D1 (bit allocation, ResNet-8 on CIFAR-10, weight-only, float 82.90 +/- 0.20 %)**
1. Budget 2.0 is degenerate: with a 2-bit floor every allocator equals uniform 2-bit (49.1 % without
   retraining, 69.6 % after the short fine-tune).
2. Above 2.0 bits and without retraining, Fisher-based per-channel allocation (`wf`, `wf_global`) beats
   per-layer allocation by +0.2 to +0.65 points on 4-5 of 5 seeds; the layer-local `baq` rule is 0.6-1.8
   points worse. After a short fine-tune all gaps except `baq` at 3.0 bits are within noise.
3. Uniform 3-bit is **worse** than every mixed allocation at 3.0 bits here (by about 4 points without
   retraining, 1-1.5 points after fine-tune). This is the opposite of the earlier lab-server result
   (AdaRound, ResNet-20, CIFAR-100). The pipelines differ (smaller net, round-to-nearest + BatchNorm
   re-estimation instead of AdaRound, first and last layers quantized), so this does not contradict it,
   but it means the "2-bit channels collapse" effect is not reproduced in this harness.
4. Diagnostic (a): single-channel loss follows the 4^(-b) law approximately from 8 down to 3 bits
   (ratios between adjacent bit-widths 2.3-5.5, noisy), and is steeper from 3 to 2 bits (ratio 6.4):
   a 2-bit channel costs about 1.6x more than the law predicts. The Fisher prediction underestimates
   the absolute loss by a roughly constant factor of 10-20 at all bit-widths, which does not change the allocation.
5. Diagnostic (b): **no superadditivity found.** Loss when 8 channels are quantized jointly, divided by the
   sum of their individual losses, is 0.82-1.09 within a layer and 1.01-1.07 across layers, at every
   bit-width including 2. Whole network vs sum of whole layers: 0.89 at 2 bits, 1.18 at 3 bits (seed range
   0.68-2.13). The loss is close to additive at this scale. This removes most of the motivation for an
   interaction-aware allocator in this harness; the only systematic departure from the additive model
   is the under-pricing of 2-bit channels in (a).

## Method details

**D3 harness (`d3/snn.py`).** SHD, first 1.0 s, dt = 10 ms (100 steps), 700 channels binned to 140
(spike counts). One recurrent layer of 128 current-based LIF units, per-neuron membrane time constant and
threshold, soft reset, fast-sigmoid surrogate gradient, leaky-integrator readout, cross-entropy on the
time-averaged readout. 36,864 weights (140x128 + 128x128 + 128x20). Adam, one-cycle schedule, 30 epochs,
batch 128. Validation: fixed stratified 10 % of the training set (815 samples); reported test accuracy is
taken at the best-validation epoch. SynOps = hidden spikes x fan-out (128 recurrent + 20 readout);
energy = SynOps x 0.9 pJ. Input-synapse operations are identical for all models and not included.
Tuning (seed 100, validation only): time constant 20 ms and learning rate 5e-3 were chosen for the
homogeneous baseline (`results/phase1/d3_tune.json`). `random_heterogeneous` samples fixed time constants
from Gamma(3, 20/3 ms); `learned_tau` trains all 128 time constants (5x learning rate on log tau).
Note the validation set shares speakers with training while the test set has two unseen speakers; validation
accuracy (about 91 %) is much higher than test (about 74 %), and augmentation that helps test hurts validation.

**D1 harness (`d1/quant.py`).** ResNet-8 (one basic block per stage, widths 16/32/64, option-A shortcuts),
15 epochs SGD, 45k/5k train/validation split. Weight-only quantization of all 7 conv layers and the fc layer
(74,800 weights); activations stay float. Symmetric per-output-channel quantizer, b bits = integer levels
+/-(2^(b-1)-1) (2 bits is ternary), MSE-optimal clipping per channel. Budget = fan-in-weighted average bits.
Sensitivity = diagonal empirical Fisher (2,048 training images, mini-batches of 8) x dw^2. Allocators:
- `uniform`: same bits everywhere (integer budgets only).
- `per_layer`: one bit-width per layer, greedy on the layer-summed Fisher x dw(b)^2 table.
- `hawq_style`: one bit-width per layer, cost = mean Hessian trace (Hutchinson, 16 probes) x ||Q_b(W)-W||^2.
- `wf`: closed-form water-filling with one global theta, b_i = clip(round(0.5 log2(c_i/(theta f_i))), 2, 8).
- `baq`: closed form R_j = round(max(0, 0.5 log2(C_j/lambda))), floor 2, lambda by bisection, with a
  layer-local sensitivity (input second moment x range^2/12, relative to layer output power). This is our
  reading of BAQ; with the task-loss Fisher the rule would be identical to `wf`.
- `wf_global`: additive allocation free to move budget anywhere, greedy on the measured per-channel
  Fisher x dw(b)^2 table (no 4^(-b) law).
Evaluation without retraining is **round-to-nearest plus BatchNorm re-estimation on 2,000 training images**
(AdaRound was not implemented in Phase 1). The short fine-tune is 100 SGD steps of quantization-aware
training with a straight-through estimator.

Augmentation lowers validation accuracy and raises test accuracy, so it cannot be selected on our

## What this means for Phase 2
- D3: the allocator can be tested, but its ceiling in this harness is the +1.35 point gain of `learned_tau`,
  and the energy comparison must use the rate-penalty curve as the reference.
- D1: there is no superadditivity to exploit at this scale. A coupled allocator has to show a gain that
  additive allocation on the measured table (`wf_global`) does not already give.
